# Escalation Guidelines

## When to Escalate

### Immediate Escalation (L1 → L2)
- Customer explicitly requests supervisor/manager
- Fraud suspected (unauthorized access, identity theft)
- Account security breach
- Transaction amount exceeds $1,000 in dispute
- Legal threats or regulatory complaints mentioned
- Customer is a VIP/Premium account holder with complex issue

### Escalation After Attempt (L1 → L2)
- Unable to resolve after 2 attempts
- System limitations prevent resolution
- Policy exception needed
- Cross-department coordination required

### L2 → L3 Escalation
- Regulatory investigation required
- Media/PR risk identified
- Executive office involvement needed
- Complex legal or compliance issue
- Customer is institutional client

## How to Escalate

### Warm Transfer (Preferred)
1. Inform customer: "I'm going to connect you with a specialist who can better assist"
2. Brief the receiving agent on:
   - Customer name and account
   - Issue summary
   - What you've already tried
   - Customer's emotional state
3. Stay on line until handoff is complete
4. Document the escalation in case notes

### Cold Transfer (When Necessary)
1. Inform customer: "I'm transferring you to our specialist team"
2. Provide case/reference number to customer
3. Add detailed notes to case before transfer
4. Set callback expectation if wait time is long

## De-escalation Techniques

Before escalating, try:

### 1. Acknowledge and Empathize
- "I understand how frustrating this must be"
- "You're right to be concerned about this"
- "I would feel the same way in your situation"

### 2. Take Ownership
- "Let me personally make sure this gets resolved"
- "I'm going to stay with you until we figure this out"

### 3. Offer Alternatives
- "While I can't do X, I can offer Y"
- "Let me see what options we have"

### 4. Set Clear Expectations
- "Here's exactly what will happen next"
- "You'll receive an update by [specific time]"

## VIP/Priority Customer Handling

### Identification
- Account tier shows "Premium" or "VIP"
- Combined balance > $100,000
- Account age > 10 years
- Relationship manager assigned

### Special Handling
- Longer hold threshold before escalation
- Higher fee waiver authority
- Direct line to L2 support
- Callback option instead of hold
- Expedited case resolution (24-48 hours)

## Escalation Documentation

Every escalation must include:
1. **Escalation Reason**: Clear categorization
2. **Customer Summary**: 2-3 sentence issue summary
3. **Attempted Resolution**: What was tried
4. **Customer Expectation**: What they want
5. **Urgency Level**: Low/Medium/High/Critical
6. **Callback Preference**: Phone/Email/Either

## Metrics and Accountability

### Healthy Escalation Rate
- L1 → L2: 10-15% of contacts
- L2 → L3: 5-10% of L2 contacts

### Red Flags (Review Needed)
- Individual agent escalation rate > 25%
- Repeat escalations for same customer
- Escalation without documented attempt
- "Supervisor request" as only reason

## Post-Escalation Follow-up

### L1 Responsibilities
- Check case status within 24 hours
- Follow up with customer if resolution delayed
- Document lessons learned

### L2 Responsibilities
- Resolve within 48 hours or communicate delay
- Provide feedback to L1 if escalation was preventable
- Update knowledge base if new issue pattern
